## 1. Scientific question

**Can different numerical implementations produce consistent solutions to the similarity equations, and how closely do the computed results agree with the published evidence?**

The study reduces the dynamics of an isothermal gas sphere to coupled ordinary differential equations (ODEs). This project examines selected numerical solutions, records comparisons between implementations, and identifies the evidence still required for a broader assessment.

## 2. Governing equations

Using the similarity variables \(x\), \(v(x)\), and \(\alpha(x)\), define

\[
w=x-v,\qquad D=w^2-1.
\]

The equations used in this computational study are

\[
xD\frac{dv}{dx}=(\alpha wx-2)w,
\]

\[
xD\frac{d\alpha}{dx}
=\alpha(\alpha x-2w)w.
\]

The dimensionless enclosed mass is calculated as

\[
m(x)=x^2\alpha(x)(x-v(x)).
\]

The equations, variable conventions, and their correspondence to the original paper must be checked as part of independent review. Agreement between numerical implementations does not by itself validate the shared mathematical formulation.

## 3. Numerical approaches

The primary numerical methods in this record are:

- **Fixed-step RK4:** classical fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration.
- **Adaptive RK45 / ODE45:** adaptive Runge–Kutta integration.
- **Higher-order Taylor series:** integration using local Taylor expansions rather than Runge–Kutta stepping or global collocation.

The methods provide complementary numerical comparisons, but the independence of each comparison must be assessed carefully. In particular, RK4 and RK45 use the same governing equations, outer asymptotic initial conditions, and endpoint mass correction in the documented collapse case.

### Scope exclusion

Chebyshev spectral collocation was explored during development but is excluded from the primary verification results and verdict. Its historical source files and diagnostic artifacts are retained as exploratory work. They are not used to support the conclusions presented here, and this record makes no claim of successful spectral convergence.

Numerical comparisons must document their initialization, domain, settings, endpoint treatment, and comparison procedure rather than relying on agreement in a single final number.

## 4. Quantitative results

### 4.1 Fixed-step RK4 versus adaptive RK45

For the collapse case with \(A=2.4\), the recorded calculations report:

| Quantity | Result |
|---|---:|
| RK4 final mass | 1.885209332164 |
| RK45 final mass | 1.885209332537 |
| Absolute difference in final mass | Approximately \(3.73\times10^{-10}\) |
| Relative difference in final mass | Approximately \(1.978\times10^{-10}\) |
| Maximum relative difference in \(\alpha\) | \(1.188383\times10^{-8}\) |
| Maximum relative difference in \(v\) | \(2.026988\times10^{-9}\) |
| Shu's rounded Table 1 value | Approximately 1.88 |

Both calculations integrate inward from \(x=10\) to \(x=10^{-3}\).

**RK4 configuration:** fixed step \(h=10^{-5}\), with 999,901 reported grid points.

**RK45 configuration:** relative tolerance \(10^{-11}\), absolute tolerance \(10^{-13}\), and maximum step \(0.005\), with 9,269 reported points.

The profile differences were calculated on a common logarithmic grid using piecewise cubic Hermite interpolation (`pchip`).

The final masses are consistent with one another and with the rounded value reported in Shu's Table 1. Since the published value is rounded, its additional decimal digits cannot be used as an exact reference for estimating numerical error.

**Scope of this comparison:** RK4 and RK45 show strong numerical consistency for the documented \(A=2.4\) configuration. Because they share the equations, initial conditions, and endpoint correction, this comparison does not independently validate those shared choices.

### 4.2 Higher-order Taylor series versus ODE45

A local integration test compared the Taylor-series method with ODE45 over the interval \(x=2\) to \(x=1.5\).

| Quantity | Maximum scaled difference |
|---|---:|
| \(v\) | \(3.585\times10^{-14}\) |
| \(\alpha\) | \(5.909\times10^{-14}\) |
| \(m\) | \(1.095\times10^{-13}\) |

For this comparison, the recorded ODE45 settings were relative tolerance \(10^{-12}\) and absolute tolerance \(10^{-14}\). The Taylor-series comparison used order \(K=16\).

The results show close numerical agreement for this particular local test. They are **not** a global error bound and do not establish agreement across the entire collapse solution or every solution branch.

To make this comparison independently reproducible, the record must link the exact Taylor implementation, initialization, comparison script, output data, and settings used in the run.

### How to interpret the comparison

The published mass value is rounded. The additional digits in the computed results should not be interpreted as a direct comparison against an exact value supplied by Shu.

The close agreement between RK4 and RK45 indicates that the two implementations produce consistent results under the tested configuration. However, both methods use the same formulation and related numerical setup. Their agreement alone cannot exclude a shared error in the equations, initialization, or endpoint correction.

The Taylor-series test provides a separate numerical comparison over a limited interval. Its local agreement with ODE45 strengthens the evidence for that test but should not be extrapolated to the full solution without further tests.

**Scope of the paper comparison:** the evidence supports numerical consistency between the tested implementations and consistency with the published rounded mass value. It does not establish that every result, solution branch, or physical conclusion in the original paper has been independently verified.

## 7. Interpretation and limitations

Agreement between numerical implementations is useful evidence, but it does not by itself establish the correctness of the governing equations, boundary conditions, physical assumptions, or interpretation.

The assessment distinguishes:

1. Agreement between numerical implementations.
2. Convergence and sensitivity to numerical settings.
3. Agreement with specific results reported in the publication.
4. Physical and mathematical consistency.
5. Independent review of the code and evidence.

Results from different methods must not be treated as directly comparable unless their configurations, definitions, and comparison domains justify it.

The principal limitations of the current evidence are:

- RK4 and RK45 share the same governing equations, outer asymptotic initial conditions, and endpoint mass correction.
- The Taylor-series comparison covers only the local interval \(x=2\) to \(x=1.5\).
- The published mass value is rounded and is not an exact high-precision reference.
- The exact scripts, raw outputs, and run settings must be linked to each quantitative claim.
- Quantitative comparisons of the density and velocity profiles against the publication remain to be documented.
- Independent review has not yet been completed.

## 9. Discussion and interpretation

Numerical reproduction requires more than translating equations into executable code. A paper may leave some implementation details unspecified, and different reasonable interpretations can produce different numerical results. This section documents the choices made in this reproduction and provides a framework for examining their implications.

### Interpretation of the original paper

The implementation follows the similarity equations associated with Shu (1977). The interpretation of the equations, solution branch, asymptotic conditions, and sonic critical point determines which solution is computed.

Any ambiguity in the original formulation should be documented explicitly. An implementation choice should not be presented as an instruction stated by the original authors unless the paper actually specifies it.

### Boundary conditions, parameters, and numerical choices

The following details should be recorded for every result used in a comparison:

- **Solution and parameters:** selected solution branch, parameter values, and any limiting procedure, such as approaching \(A=2\) from above.
- **Starting conditions:** starting point and the values or asymptotic expansions used to initialize the integration.
- **Integration domain:** initial and final similarity coordinates and the direction of integration.
- **Critical-point treatment:** how regularity conditions and the sonic critical point are handled.
- **Numerical settings:** method, order, tolerances, step-size controls, and relevant convergence tests.
- **Output and comparison:** definition of each reported quantity, evaluation point or domain, and procedure used to calculate differences.

Where information is not specified in the original paper, it should be marked **Not reported**. Where the implementation makes a choice, that choice and its rationale should be documented separately.

### Discussion of quantitative discrepancies

Differences between computed quantities should be retained as numerical evidence rather than removed by rounding. Their significance depends on the quantity, numerical setup, and scientific purpose.

Potential explanations for discrepancies include differences in initialization, integration domains, numerical approximations, interpretation of the equations, or the precision of published reference values. These are hypotheses to investigate, not established explanations unless supported by specific tests.

Observational uncertainties may provide scientific context, but they do not replace a numerical comparison against the original result.

### Physical interpretation

The reproduced solutions have been examined for qualitative consistency with the behavior considered in this benchmark. This assessment is distinct from a claim of exact quantitative agreement.

The record should identify which physical features were tested, what evidence supports each interpretation, and which conclusions remain untested. Agreement between numerical methods supports confidence in the computed solution but does not by itself establish the correctness of the physical model or every conclusion of the original paper.

### Community discussion

This record is intended to support critical, constructive examination of numerical research. Readers and contributors are encouraged to identify missing implementation details, question assumptions, suggest alternative interpretations, and submit independent comparisons.

Discussion should refer to specific equations, parameters, code versions, numerical results, or passages in the original paper whenever possible. Substantive corrections should be incorporated into the evidence record with their provenance preserved.

## 10. Current verdict

**Verdict: Partial numerical reproduction — broader verification pending.**

The current evidence establishes close numerical consistency between RK4 and RK45 for the documented \(A=2.4\) collapse calculation. It also establishes close agreement between the Taylor-series method and ODE45 on the specified local test interval. The computed collapse mass is consistent with the rounded value reported in Shu's Table 1.

These results are meaningful but limited. They do not independently validate the shared equations and setup used by RK4 and RK45, establish global accuracy of the Taylor-series solution, or verify every numerical result and physical conclusion in the original paper.

The verdict should be revised only when additional reproducible evidence, direct comparisons, sensitivity tests, and independent reviews justify a broader claim.
