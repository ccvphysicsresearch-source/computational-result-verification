## 1. Scientific question

**Can independent numerical methods produce consistent solutions to the similarity equations, and how well do the computed results agree with the published evidence?**

The study reduces the dynamics of an isothermal gas sphere to coupled ordinary differential equations. This project examines the numerical solutions using different computational approaches and records their agreement, convergence behavior, and remaining uncertainties.

## 2. Governing equations

Using the similarity variables \(x\), \(v(x)\), and \(\alpha(x)\), define

\[
w=x-v,\qquad D=w^2-1.
\]

The equations used in this computational study are

\[
xD\frac{dv}{dx}=(\alpha wx-2)w,
\]

\[
xD\frac{d\alpha}{dx}
=\alpha(\alpha x-2w)w.
\]

The dimensionless enclosed mass is calculated as

\[
m(x)=x^2\alpha(x)(x-v(x)).
\]

These equations and their conventions must be checked against the original publication as part of the independent review.

## 3. Numerical approaches

The project has explored several methods:

- **ODE45:** adaptive Runge–Kutta integration.
- **Higher-order Taylor series:** integration using local Taylor expansions.
- **Fixed-step RK4:** a historical comparison method.
- **Chebyshev spectral collocation:** a global discretization method whose earlier experiments showed convergence limitations.

Different methods may use different starting points, domains, tolerances, or assumptions. Comparisons must therefore document their configurations rather than relying on agreement in a single final number.

| Quantity | Maximum scaled difference |

### 4.3 Spectral-method experiments

The earlier Chebyshev calculation at \(N=240\) reported a final mass of 1.8851668012. The \(N=320\) run stagnated and is **not considered converged**.

The spectral result is retained as historical evidence, not as a confirmed reference value. Differences in starting points, domains, and numerical configurations must be examined before comparing its final mass directly with the time-marching results.

### How to interpret the comparison

The published value \(1.88\) is rounded. The additional digits reported by our implementations should therefore not be interpreted as a direct comparison against an exact numerical value supplied by Shu.

The close agreement between RK4 and ODE45 indicates that these two implementations produce consistent results for the tested configuration. It is a separate question whether the remaining differences from other reported or independently computed values arise from numerical error, different starting conditions, integration domains, parameter choices, or interpretation of the original formulation.

The reproduced solutions are qualitatively consistent with the physical behavior discussed in Shu (1977), within the scope of the cases examined. A stronger quantitative assessment requires each comparison to identify the corresponding published result, the precise definition of the quantity, and the numerical conditions under which it was obtained.

**Scope of this comparison:** the evidence supports numerical cross-consistency between the tested implementations and agreement with the published rounded mass value. It should not be taken as proof that every result or conclusion in the original paper has been independently verified.

## 7. Interpretation and limitations

Agreement between independently implemented numerical methods is useful evidence, but it does not by itself prove that the governing equations, boundary conditions, physical assumptions, or interpretation are correct.

The final assessment must distinguish:

1. Agreement between implementations.
2. Convergence and sensitivity to numerical settings.
3. Agreement with specific results reported in the publication.
4. Physical and mathematical consistency.
5. Independent review of the code and evidence.

Results from different methods must not be treated as directly comparable unless their configurations and comparison domains justify it.

## 9. Discussion and interpretation

Numerical reproduction requires more than translating equations into executable code. A paper may leave some implementation details unspecified, and different reasonable interpretations can produce quantitatively different results. This section documents the choices made in this reproduction and provides a place to discuss their implications.

### Interpretation of the original paper

The implementation follows the similarity equations associated with Shu (1977). The interpretation of the governing equations, solution branch, asymptotic conditions, and sonic critical point determines which solution is computed.

Any ambiguity in the original formulation should be documented explicitly. An implementation choice should not be presented as an instruction stated by the original authors unless the paper actually specifies it.

### Boundary conditions, parameters, and numerical choices

The following details should be recorded for every result used in a comparison:

- **Solution and parameters:** the selected solution branch, parameter values, and any limiting procedure, such as approaching \(A=2\) from above.
- **Starting conditions:** the starting point and the values or asymptotic expansions used to initialize the integration.
- **Integration domain:** the initial and final values of the similarity coordinate and the direction of integration.
- **Critical-point treatment:** how regularity conditions and the sonic critical point are handled.
- **Numerical settings:** solver or method, order, tolerances, step-size controls, and any relevant convergence tests.
- **Output and comparison:** the definition of each reported quantity, the evaluation point or domain, and the procedure used to calculate differences.

Where information is not specified in the original paper, it should be marked **Not reported**. Where our implementation makes a choice, that choice and its rationale should be documented separately.

### Discussion of quantitative discrepancies

Some computed quantities differ at approximately the third or fourth significant figure. These differences are retained as part of the numerical evidence rather than removed by rounding.

Potential explanations include differences in initialization, integration domains, numerical approximations, interpretation of the original equations, or the precision of published reference values. These are possibilities to investigate, not established explanations unless supported by specific tests.

The significance of a discrepancy depends on the quantity being considered. A difference may be negligible for one scientific purpose but important for another. Observational uncertainties can provide useful context, but they vary across astronomical measurements and do not replace a numerical comparison against the original result.

### Physical interpretation

The reproduced solutions are qualitatively consistent with the physical behavior examined in this benchmark. This assessment is distinct from a claim of exact quantitative agreement.

The record should identify which physical features were tested, what evidence supports each interpretation, and which conclusions remain untested. Agreement between numerical methods supports confidence in the computed solution but does not, by itself, establish the correctness of the physical model or every conclusion of the original paper.

## 10. Current verdict

**Assessment: Qualitative consistency demonstrated for the cases examined; quantitative verification remains scoped to the comparisons documented here.**

The tested independent implementations show strong numerical consistency for the reported quantities, and the reproduced collapse solution is qualitatively consistent with the physical behavior discussed in Shu (1977). The reported dimensionless mass is also consistent with the rounded value shown in Shu's Table 1.

These findings do not establish that every numerical result, solution branch, or physical conclusion in the original paper has been independently verified. Quantitative discrepancies, implementation choices, and limitations must remain visible in the record.

The verdict may be revised as additional direct comparisons, independent reviews, or reproducible evidence become available.
